FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Answers to Common Borewell Questions

Clear engineering guidance so you can make informed decisions about your property.

We conduct an initial satellite reconnaissance followed by an on-site geophysical resistivity survey. This detects subterranean water-bearing fractures, rock strata thickness, and potential aquifer yield before any drilling machinery arrives.

Borewell depth depends entirely on local geology and aquifer levels. In urban residential areas, depth typically ranges between 350 to 600 feet, while in agricultural and hard granite zones, depths may reach 700 to 1,200 feet.

With our modern high-pressure hydraulic DTH rigs, drilling a standard 500-foot borewell is usually completed in 6 to 10 hours under normal hard-rock conditions.

Pump selection is based on the total head (depth of borewell + elevation to storage tank) and water discharge volume. Multi-stage stainless steel submersible pumps are most reliable for deep borewells. We calculate exact HP requirements after conducting the flow yield test.

Yes. We collect water samples immediately after drilling and send them to an accredited testing laboratory to evaluate Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), pH, hardness, nitrates, and bacterial safety.

Yes. We provide high-pressure compressor flushing, desilting, pump retrieving, electrical panel servicing, and annual maintenance contracts for homes, apartments, and factories.

Borewell drilling costs depend on drilling depth per foot, casing pipe length and thickness, soil condition, and pump horsepower. We provide clear, itemized quotes after our site inspection visit with no hidden surcharges.
